## 3. An operational identification sequence

Treat GM as a relation between a wording, its meaning, and a plausible congruent agnate. Use the following sequence rather than suffix spotting:

1. **Establish the contextual meaning.** Determine what experience, logical relation, speech function, modal assessment, or polarity is being realized in this co-text.
2. **Construct a plausible congruent agnate.** Preserve the relevant meaning as far as possible; do not invent an unnatural paraphrase merely to force a contrast.
3. **Compare semantic category with grammatical realization.** Ask whether a process, quality, circumstance, relator, speech function, or modal value is being realized through a non-default grammatical resource.
4. **Test semantic junction.** Ask whether the metaphorical wording combines the original semantic value with affordances of a second category, such as a process construed with thing-like definiteness, modification, quantification, or thematic potential.
5. **Locate rank and degree of realization.** Record whether the shift is from clause complex to clause, clause to group, group to word, or another relation; distinguish full, intermediate, and raw realization when that distinction is supported.
6. **Check morphology without making it decisive.** Prefer a direct morphological agnate when available, but recognize non-morphological agnation such as `if` → `condition` or `can` → `possibility`.
7. **Interpret the functional consequence.** Explain changes in agency, time, causality, negotiability, arguability, information flow, abstraction, and technicality.
8. **Report uncertainty.** Treat lexicalized, technicalized, or weakly agnate cases as gradient or disputed when the evidence does not justify a binary decision.

This sequence synthesizes the presentation's emphasis on semantic junction, rank shift, full realization, morphological priority, and context. Yang 2026, slides 54-119; Yang 2020; Li & Yang 2024.

## 4. Rank shift, process shift, and embedding

Keep three questions distinct.

### Process-type change

A change in the transitivity label of a Process is not sufficient evidence of GM. In `The fifth day saw them at the summit`, the important contrast with `They arrived at the summit on the fifth day` is not simply that `see` is Mental and `arrive` Material. The time circumstance is reconstrued as a participant-like point of departure, creating a semantic junction through a rank/category realignment. Yang notes that this example encouraged a process-shift misreading and was removed from later IFG editions. Yang 2026, slides 54-65.

### Rank shift

Rank shift is an important realization tendency for ideational GM, but it is not a universal sufficient condition. It does not explain mood or modality metaphor, and it does not make every embedded clause metaphorical. Yang 2026, slides 65-69.

### Embedding

An embedded relative, projected complement, WH-clause, or non-finite clause may retain a congruent clausal construal. Diagnose GM only when compression and semantic junction accompany the embedding.

Compare:

```text
the man [[who arrived yesterday]]
his arrival yesterday
```

The first contains a rank-shifted clause but preserves verbal process realization. The second construes arriving through a thing-like nominal group. Under Yang's Full Realization Principle, only full compression in both meaning and form counts as ideational GM; intermediate realization is partly metaphorical, while raw realization is not counted as GM. Yang 2020, especially printed pp. 161, 166; Yang 2026, slides 69-86.

Do not generalize this full-realization test to all GM types. Interpersonal metaphor is identified through contextual speech function or modal value, not through rank shift alone.

## 5. Nominalizing metaphor as a system of choices

Do not ask only whether a word is morphologically nominalized. Treat nominalizing metaphor (NM) as a stratal realignment described by four complementary systems:

| System | Core question | Representative choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Semantic junction | What meaning is joined with entity meaning? | quality + entity; process + entity; circumstance + entity; relator + entity |
| Morphological priority | How directly are the agnates related in form? | derivational; inflectional/infinitival; non-morphological |
| Full realization | How completely is compression realized in meaning and form? | full; intermediate; raw |
| Rank shift | What rank relation realizes the reconstrual? | clause complex → clause/group/word; clause → group/word |

Use the systems together. None of them alone identifies all NMs. Li & Yang 2024; Yang 2026, slides 87-102, especially slides 97, 101-102.

Recognize non-morphological NMs. Examples discussed in the presentation include:

```text
can / could → possibility / potential
want to → desire
if → condition
so → cause / reason
with → accompaniment
to → destination
```

Do not automatically classify the following as active NM: technicalized terms, event nouns, participant nouns, and finite or non-finite nominal clauses. Test a recoverable congruent agnate, semantic junction, context, and current usage. Lexicalization and technicalization are gradient; avoid declaring a metaphor “dead” from intuition alone.

## 6. Interpersonal metaphor: context first

Determine the enacted speech function before comparing it with Mood. A declarative, interrogative, or imperative is not inherently congruent or metaphorical outside context.

Use Yang's two principles:

- **Context-first Principle:** infer the operative speech function or modal force from the situation, co-text, participant roles, and response potential before classifying the realization.
- **AS IF Principle:** identify an interpersonal metaphor when an utterance with one grammatical value is being used as if it realized the congruent value of another; compare the default congruent utterance with the metaphorical counterpart.

For mood metaphor:

1. Identify whether the exchange gives or demands information or goods-and-services.
2. Use the response potential as evidence: an answer, compliance, acceptance, rejection, or challenge helps reveal the enacted function.
3. Compare the function with the unmarked Mood realization.
4. Explain the effect as a change in negotiability, arguability, authority, politeness, or distance; do not reduce all indirectness to politeness.

For modality metaphor, establish the value and orientation that the clause enacts in context. `I think ...` can realize probability, but in professional advice it may indirectly enact obligation. Do not classify it from the projecting clause alone.

Source lineage: Yang 2019, pp. 186-203, especially p. 194; Yang 2026, slides 103-119. Treat the principles as Yang's refinement, not Halliday's terminology.

## 7. Logical, textual, polarity, and contextual extensions

### Logical grammatical metaphor

Use `logical grammatical metaphor` as a useful later analytic label for the metaphorical realization of logico-semantic relations, while keeping its status explicit. Halliday's ideational metafunction includes experiential and logical components, but he did not consistently establish logical GM as a third top-level GM category. Later SFL work, including Martin's, gives the label greater prominence.

Typical movements include:

```text
so → cause / result
if → condition
then → follow / subsequent
although → concession / despite
```

State whether the analysis treats logical GM as a subtype of ideational GM or as a separate descriptive category. Yang argues that the label is terminologically useful and helps observation, not that it is an undisputed Hallidayan category. Yang 2026, slides 120-130.

### Textual metaphor

Treat textual metaphor as contested. Martin (1992) proposes resources such as meta-message relation, text reference, negotiating texture, and internal conjunction. Yang (2018) argues that the category lacks a stable congruent baseline, overlaps with ideational metaphor, relies heavily on nominalization, has weak identification principles, and lacks a uniquely established function.

Do not label clefts, passives, internal conjunction, or text-reference nouns as textual metaphor merely because they affect information flow. First specify the proposed congruent textual realization and show why the contrast cannot be explained more adequately as an ideational or interpersonal metaphor with textual effects. Yang 2018, pp. 205-222; Yang 2026, slides 131-142.

### Polarity metaphor

Do not confine polarity metaphor to the interpersonal metafunction. Halliday's 1984 example `I don't think he's coming` contains both a modal metaphor and a polarity metaphor: the negative is transferred into the projecting clause. Halliday, `Grammatical Metaphor in English and Chinese` (printed p. 329; PDF p. 340).

Yang & Gao broaden polarity metaphor into the indirect reconstrual of negative meaning with both ideational and interpersonal realizations:

- **Ideational:** participation, qualification, processation, and circumstantiation.
- **Interpersonal:** tension involving mood and speech function, or varied realizations of modality.

Analyze polarity as a cline where relevant, not only as a binary `not`. Keep Halliday's original interpersonal observation distinct from Yang & Gao's later cross-metafunctional model. Yang & Gao 2023, article 103623; Yang 2026, slides 143-155.

### Contextual metaphor

Treat contextual metaphor as a plausible but underdeveloped extension, not a settled Hallidayan category. If used, specify the contextual variables and realization relation involved. Test field, tenor, and mode rather than speaking vaguely of “context,” and identify whether the proposed tension is between genre and register, between contextual parameters, or between context and discourse semantics. Yang 2026, slides 156-161.

Use the same caution for multimodal or semiotic metaphor: name the semiotic strata and resources being compared, and cite the later scholar responsible for the extension.

## 8. Powers and applications

GM expands meaning potential in several interlocking ways:

- **Semantic:** creates additional ways of construing experience and relations.
- **Cognitive:** supports abstraction, reasoning, conceptual stabilization, and theory construction.
- **Discoursal:** compresses information and supports dense, recursive, cohesive argument.
- **Epistemological:** objectifies, accumulates, transmits, and reuses disciplinary knowledge.
- **Interpersonal:** expands options for command, offer, stance, commitment, obligation, polarity, negotiability, and authority.

Do not describe the “power of GM” only through nominalization. The presentation explicitly criticizes accounts that omit mood, modality, and polarity metaphor. Yang 2026, slides 27-34, 196-200.

Route applications by the problem being investigated:

| Problem family | Domains | Main GM questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge construction and learning | science, academic writing, disciplinary discourse, history, schooling, L2/EAP/CLIL | How are processes packaged as concepts, causal networks built, and novice-expert development traced? |
| Power and institutional action | critical, political, legal, and medical discourse | How are agency, obligation, legitimacy, authority, and contestability redistributed? |
| Cross-lingual reconstruction | translation and multilingual production | Which congruent/metaphorical relations are preserved, unpacked, explicated, or reconfigured across languages? |
| Empirical modelling | corpus linguistics and historical change | How frequent, dispersed, register-sensitive, and diachronically variable are the resources under an explicit annotation scheme? |
| Cross-semiotic knowledge | multimodal science, mathematics, textbooks, infographics | How do linguistic, visual, mathematical, and other resources jointly reconstrue meaning? |
| Computational support | NLP, automated detection, writing feedback, readability | Which GM definition is operationalized, how is gold data annotated, and what errors arise from nominalization proxies? |
| Interaction and identity | conversation, professional advice, digital discourse | How do mood, modality, and polarity negotiate compliance, distance, stance, identity, and dialogic space? |

Avoid using GM as a synonym for complexity, sophistication, objectivity, or ideology. Measure the actual realization and compare alternatives. Yang 2026, slides 165-200.

## 9. Research agenda and AI-data caution

Treat the following as open problems rather than solved extensions:

- defining congruence and degrees of reconstrual;
- reproducible identification across GM types;
- lexicalization and the lexical-grammatical boundary;
- a unified interpersonal account;
- the status of textual, contextual, and multimodal metaphor;
- cross-linguistic comparison without English templates;
- shared corpus annotation standards;
- computational detection beyond nominalization;
- links with cognition and processing.

The presentation proposes a future general theory of meaning reconstrual that integrates multiple domains along degrees rather than binary labels. Present this as a research programme, not current consensus. Yang 2026, slides 162-164.

Treat LLM-generated examples as potentially useful hypotheses but contaminated linguistic evidence. Do not count them as naturally occurring corpus data without explicit provenance, sampling, and separation from human text. Yang 2026, slide 201.

The presentation's Wittgenstein-SFL comparison can motivate a context-first orientation: meaning as use, language games and social activities, and forms of life and culture. Treat this as an analytical analogy, not a direct theoretical identity or genealogy. Yang 2026, slides 202-207.

## 10. Compact answer patterns

### Historical question

> The answer depends on the milestone: 1966 provides the conceptual precursor, 1976 contains an earlier broader use of the phrase, 1984 explicitly names and compares the mature phenomenon, and 1985 gives the canonical systematic account. The earliest explicit mature naming I can verify is 1984.

### “Is this nominalization GM?”

> Morphology alone is insufficient. I would first construct the congruent agnate, test semantic junction, record rank and realization degree, and check whether current context preserves both process/quality/relation meaning and thing-like affordances.

### “Is this embedded clause GM?”

> Embedding is not enough. The clause counts as ideational GM only if the analysis can demonstrate compression and non-congruent semantic-grammatical realization; otherwise it is ordinary rank-shifted embedding.

### “Is this indirect request a mood metaphor?”

> Establish the speech function from context and response potential, then compare it with Mood. An interrogative is metaphorical as a command only when the exchange supports the command reading.

### “Does polarity metaphor belong to one metafunction?”

> Halliday's original example is interpersonal, but Yang and Gao's later model recognizes both ideational and interpersonal polarity metaphor. State which model is being used.

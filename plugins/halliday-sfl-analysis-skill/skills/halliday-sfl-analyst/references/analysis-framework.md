# Detailed SFL analysis framework

Use this reference for full and research analyses. Apply categories as systems of contrast, not as a checklist that every text must instantiate.

## 1. Context and unit selection

### Context hypothesis

Describe evidence for field, tenor, and mode before assigning a register label. Then test the hypothesis against recurrent semantic and lexicogrammatical selections.

Useful prompts:

- What social activity is being performed, beyond the subject matter?
- What sequence of activity does the text enable or accompany?
- Which institutional and speech roles are available?
- How equal, familiar, aligned, and frequently interacting are the participants?
- Is language constitutive of the activity or ancillary to non-verbal action?
- Is interaction immediate or delayed, dialogic or monologic, spoken-like or written-like?

### Unit selection

- Use the clause as the default unit for metafunctional analysis.
- Use the clause complex for taxis, expansion, and projection.
- Use groups and phrases for participant configuration, modification, appraisal, and grammatical metaphor.
- Use paragraphs, turns, phases, and whole-text stages for discourse development.
- State how embedded and rankshifted clauses are handled in research counts.

## 2. Ideational meaning

### Experiential transitivity

Use the following roles as orientation points. Verify them from the language-specific grammar and the clause's systemic contrasts.

| Process | Central configuration | Diagnostic focus |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Actor + Process, optionally Goal/Scope/Recipient/Client/Attribute | Doing, happening, effective vs middle, agency and affectedness |
| Mental | Senser + Process + Phenomenon | Sensing, consciousness, projection, direction of sensing |
| Relational | Carrier + Attribute or Token + Value | Attribution, identification, intensive/possessive/circumstantial relation |
| Behavioral | Behaver + Process, sometimes Behavior/Phenomenon | Physiological or psychological behavior near material/mental boundaries |
| Verbal | Sayer + Process, optionally Receiver/Verbiage/Target | Symbolic exchange, attribution, projection, voice ownership |
| Existential | Process + Existent | Introducing existence or occurrence, often with reduced agency |

Treat process boundaries as regions with diagnostic evidence, not as perfectly discrete boxes. Report uncertain or hybrid cases.

For recurrent patterns, ask:

- Who receives agency, consciousness, voice, attributes, identity, or existence?
- Are human actors replaced by institutions, abstractions, systems, or nominalized processes?
- Which participants disappear in passive, middle, impersonal, or nominal constructions?
- Which circumstances of time, place, manner, cause, accompaniment, role, matter, or angle become prominent?
- Does the text favor dynamic processes or stable relations?

### Ergativity and agency

Use an ergative perspective when it adds explanatory value: distinguish Medium, through which a process is actualized, from an external Agent that instigates it. Do not force both transitive and ergative models into every clause.

### Logical relations in clause complexes

Describe:

- **Taxis**: parataxis for equal status; hypotaxis for dependency.
- **Expansion**: elaboration, extension, enhancement.
- **Projection**: locution or idea.

Explain how dependency and logico-semantic relations affect voice, evidence, causality, sequence, and attribution. Distinguish explicit conjunction from implicit rhetorical relations.

## 3. Interpersonal meaning

### Exchange and speech function

Start from the commodity and role:

| Commodity | Giving | Demanding |
|---|---|---|
| Information | statement | question |
| Goods-and-services | offer | command |

Then compare speech function with grammatical Mood. A congruent pairing is common but not mandatory; mismatches can realize politeness, indirectness, or strategic ambiguity.

### Mood and negotiability

For English, examine Subject and Finite as the Mood element and the remainder as Residue. Ask:

- Who is made responsible for the validity of the proposition?
- What is negotiable through polarity, tense, modality, or tag?
- Are commands imperative, interrogative, declarative, or elliptical?
- How do responses confirm, challenge, comply, refuse, or defer?

Use language-specific Mood systems for non-English texts.

### Modality

Distinguish:

- **Modalization** of propositions: probability and usuality.
- **Modulation** of proposals: obligation and inclination.
- **Value**: low, median, high, interpreted relative to the local system.
- **Orientation/manifestation**: subjective or objective, explicit or implicit, where the grammar supports the distinction.

Track the source of commitment. Compare a modal auxiliary, modal adjunct, projecting clause, impersonal construction, and attributed voice only when they are plausible alternatives in the language.

### Evaluation and voice

Analyze evaluative lexis, intensification, attribution, concession, denial, and projection when relevant. If using Attitude, Engagement, or Graduation, label them as Appraisal-theory terms from later SFL development, not as Halliday's own complete interpersonal model.

## 4. Textual meaning

### Theme and Rheme

Identify the first experiential element as topical Theme in English. Account for preceding textual or interpersonal Themes when present. Distinguish:

- Simple and multiple Theme.
- Unmarked and marked Theme relative to Mood and language-specific norms.
- Theme in declarative, interrogative, imperative, and dependent clauses.
- Thematic equatives or predicated Themes when they materially affect focus.

Explain the discourse consequence of thematic selection; do not stop at bracketing Theme and Rheme.

### Thematic progression

Look for tendencies such as:

- Constant Theme.
- Linear progression from prior Rheme to later Theme.
- Derived Themes organized by a hyper-Theme.
- Thematic reset at a new phase.

Treat these as descriptive patterns, not rigid templates. Connect paragraph openings and section headings to local clause Themes.

### Information structure

Distinguish information units, Given, and New from Theme and Rheme. In spoken language, use tonality and tonicity where available. In unannotated writing, make only cautious inferences from syntax, punctuation, clefting, and discourse context; do not claim intonational focus.

### Cohesion

Track reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction, and lexical cohesion. Explain what identities, contrasts, taxonomies, or activity sequences the cohesive chains maintain. Include conjunction expressed implicitly only as an interpretive inference.

## 5. Grammatical metaphor

For a judgement about one clause, group, phrase, word, or morpheme, follow [gm-identification-protocol.md](gm-identification-protocol.md) and [gm-annotation-framework-v2.md](gm-annotation-framework-v2.md). The forms below are candidate signals only; the normative item record is [gm-annotation-v2.schema.json](gm-annotation-v2.schema.json).

### Ideational metaphor

Check for:

- Process → thing, often through nominalization.
- Quality → thing or process.
- Logical relation → process, noun, or circumstance.
- Participant or circumstance → quality or classifier.
- Sequences compressed into figures and figures compressed into nominal groups.

Unpack the metaphor into one or more plausible congruent clauses. Compare changes in rank, participant roles, agency, tense, modality, causality, and the ability to qualify or classify the new entity.

For a nominalizing candidate, record all four diagnostics rather than only the nominal form:

- semantic junction: process/quality/circumstance/relator + entity;
- morphological priority: derivational, then inflectional/infinitival for a lexical gap, then non-morphological where necessary;
- realization degree: full, intermediate, or raw;
- rank relation: clause complex to clause/group/word or clause to group/word.

Only a full realization that also passes the other relevant tests supports a canonical nominalizing-GM verdict. Treat intermediate realization as gradient and raw clausal realization as non-GM. Exclude ordinary participant/product nouns, lexicalized labels, event nouns without an unpackable process, and embedded clauses unless the positive relation is independently demonstrated.

### Interpersonal metaphor

Check for:

- A projecting clause realizing modality, such as a personal or impersonal assessment.
- A declarative or interrogative realizing a command.
- Explicit-objective formulations that present a judgment as detached from the speaker.

Compare force, responsibility, negotiability, politeness, and dialogic openness. Avoid assuming indirectness always means politeness.

Establish the contextual speech function and response potential before comparing it with Mood. Apply the AS IF test and supply the congruent statement, question, command, offer, or modal wording. A surface imperative, interrogative, or declarative has no context-free metaphor status.

### Polarity and proposed textual metaphor

For polarity metaphor, identify the negative meaning and a direct congruent negative before classifying an ideational realization (participation, processation, qualification, or circumstantiation) or an interpersonal one (mood or modality tension).

Treat textual metaphor as disputed. Require a defensible congruent textual baseline, an operational contrast beyond ordinary markedness, and evidence that the case is not better analyzed as ideational/interpersonal GM with textual effects.

## 6. Multilingual safeguards

- Begin with functional questions, then identify how the particular language realizes them.
- Do not import English word order, Subject tests, tense systems, articles, or process diagnostics unchanged.
- Separate comparative hypotheses from established descriptions.
- Preserve the original text and provide glosses when the audience needs them.
- For Chinese, attend to clause chaining, aspect, modal resources, topic-prominent organization, omitted participants, coverbs/localizers, and information flow without automatically equating initial position with English Theme.
- For translation analysis, compare meaning selections and degrees of equivalence rather than aligning forms one-to-one.

## 7. Evidence and quantification

### Evidence ladder

Prefer claims supported by:

1. A recurrent pattern across the text.
2. A contrast with a plausible alternative.
3. Co-occurrence across metafunctions.
4. A comparison text, register, translation, author, period, or corpus.
5. Contextual evidence independent of the feature being interpreted.

Use a single striking example as illustration, not as proof of a broad distribution.

### Quantification

For each count, report:

- Corpus or sample size.
- Unit and inclusion/exclusion rule.
- Raw count and denominator.
- Percentage or normalized rate when useful.
- Ambiguous cases and inter-annotator procedure when applicable.

Do not call a feature frequent or significant without a baseline. Reserve statistical significance for an actual statistical test.

Return one schema-valid v2 JSON object per item, followed by the prescribed concise explanation. Keep ideational and interpersonal status independent, record polarity separately, and include both evidence and counterevidence. For nominalizing candidates, the record is incomplete without MPP candidates, selected agnate and level, status, priority check, and cross-linguistic caution where applicable.

Record:

- Segmentation convention.
- Coding system and delicacy.
- Treatment of embedded clauses, quotations, headings, fragments, and tables.
- Sampling and comparison design.
- Unresolved cases and revision history.

# Halliday SFL theory core

Use this reference to keep analyses conceptually consistent. Treat it as a compact map, not a substitute for language-specific description.

## 1. The generative kernel

Language is a social semiotic: a historically evolved meaning potential that is instantiated in text. Grammar is not primarily a set of correctness rules. It is the central resource through which people construe experience, enact social relations, and organize these meanings into unfolding discourse.

The compact model is:

```text
context of culture / context of situation
                 ⇅ realization
semantics: ideational + interpersonal + textual meaning
                 ⇅ realization
lexicogrammar: wording as systems of choice
                 ⇅ realization
phonology or graphology
                 ⇅ realization
sound, gesture, or graphic substance
```

Realization is a constitutive relation between strata, not a pipeline that preserves identical content in different codes. Analyze patterns from above, roundabout, and below.

## 2. Seven commitments

1. **Resource over rule**: Model language as networks of meaning options. Interpret a structure as the realization of selections from alternatives.
2. **Construal over reflection**: Grammar participates in categorizing experience. Different wordings can construct different agents, causes, temporalities, and entities.
3. **Simultaneous metafunctions**: Every relevant instance can construe experience, enact relations, and organize discourse at once.
4. **Lexicogrammatical continuity**: Lexis and grammar form a cline from more general, closed resources to more delicate, open resources.
5. **System-instance relation**: Text is an instance of a system; register is a subpotential associated probabilistically with a situation type.
6. **Dynamic semogenesis**: Meaning potential changes in social history, individual development, and unfolding text.
7. **Appliability**: Let practical problems test and develop the theory. Analysis must illuminate a problem, not merely display terminology.

## 3. Keep the dimensions separate

| Dimension | Terms | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Stratification | context, semantics, lexicogrammar, phonology/graphology, substance | What realizes what? |
| Metafunction | ideational, interpersonal, textual | What simultaneous mode of meaning is at stake? |
| Instantiation | system, subpotential/register, instance type, text instance | How does this use relate to the total potential? |
| Axes | paradigmatic system, syntagmatic structure | What was selected and how was it combined? |
| Rank/delicacy | clause, group/phrase, word, morpheme; general to fine-grained | At what unit and precision is the description operating? |

Do not flatten terms from different dimensions into a single inventory. For example, Theme is a lexicogrammatical system serving textual meaning; register is a region on the cline of instantiation associated with context; clause is a rank unit.

## 4. Metafunctions

### Ideational

Use the **experiential** component to describe how a language construes configurations of process, participants, and circumstances. Use the **logical** component to describe interdependency and logico-semantic relations among clauses and other complexes.

### Interpersonal

Describe how language enacts social roles and relations: exchanging information or goods-and-services, assigning responsibility to a Subject, negotiating polarity, grading commitment or obligation, attributing voices, and evaluating people and events.

### Textual

Describe how ideational and interpersonal meanings become an operational message: Theme-Rheme organization, information flow, cohesive resources, and the development of a text in its context.

Metafunctions are internal organizing principles of language, not three external intentions and not three separate sections of a sentence.

## 5. Context and register

- **Field**: the social activity and domain of experience.
- **Tenor**: participant roles, status, contact, alignment, and evaluative relations.
- **Mode**: channel, interaction, rhetorical role of language, and the division of labor between language and other activity.

Treat field, tenor, and mode as variables that probabilistically activate semantic patterns. Together they characterize register. Do not reduce register to formality or a vocabulary list.

Distinguish register from genre. Halliday supplies the context-metafunction architecture; genre as a staged, goal-oriented social process was elaborated especially in later Sydney-school work. Name that lineage when genre theory is used.

## 6. Three time scales of meaning

- **Phylogenesis/systemic evolution**: change in a community's meaning potential across social history.
- **Ontogenesis**: the individual's development from protolanguage into a stratified adult language system.
- **Logogenesis**: the creation and adjustment of meaning potential as a text unfolds.

Use these scales to avoid treating a grammar as static. A community makes available the system a child learns; children reconstruct it through interaction; accumulated instances can maintain or change it.

## 7. Grammatical metaphor

Grammatical metaphor is a non-congruent relation between semantics and lexicogrammar, not ornamental vocabulary. In ideational metaphor, processes and qualities are often reconstrued as things, while logical relations are compressed inside clauses. In interpersonal metaphor, modality or speech function may be realized through a clause rather than a more congruent modal or Mood choice.

Example:

```text
The committee rejected the proposal, so the market reacted sharply.
→ The committee's rejection of the proposal caused a sharp market reaction.
```

The second form can create technicality and reasoning density, but may background time, agency, and contestability. Always compare plausible alternatives before assigning an ideological effect.

## 8. Complementarity

Retain both sides of each pair:

- System and instance.
- Choice and structure.
- Grammar and lexis.
- Speech and writing.
- Congruent and metaphorical realization.
- Potential and probability.
- Material realization and semiotic meaning.

Hallidayan analysis gains depth by rotating perspectives rather than choosing one side as the whole explanation.

## 9. Boundaries and common errors

- Do not treat transitivity as verb valency alone.
- Do not equate Theme with grammatical Subject or an everyday topic.
- Do not infer Given/New solely from first and last position in a written clause.
- Do not treat grammatical metaphor as unusual literary language.
- Do not equate function with a speaker's conscious intention.
- Do not present English systems as ready-made universals.
- Do not attribute Appraisal, genre pedagogy, or later discourse-semantic networks to Halliday alone.
- Do not infer ideology from a single passive or nominalization; require patterns, contrasts, and context.
